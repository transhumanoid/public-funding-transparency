# Identity and Destination Binding

## BSB and account matching

The quiet genius: boring, bureaucratic, almost impossible to fake at scale because the destination is a real institution with a real identity behind it.

A local primary school claiming money for a playground upgrade has the BSB and the account number for the primary school, and it has to match. Someone claiming community funds and the money going somewhere else just won't cut it. The crypto layer just becomes the courier — the real gate is the identity binding on both ends.

You could only move it from one authored account or one approved account to another.

## Nudge paths

You could push it, prompt it, nudge it:

- An email comes through officially from the head of the school — the headmaster, the chief of accounts — going "hang on"
- It gets flagged by members of the community

Each nudge path authenticated differently so no single forged email can spring a blocked payment. But that loops you right back to identity — the nudge is only as trustworthy as whoever's sending it, which means the school still needs a verified key on file. You're not escaping verification, you're distributing it.

## Keys for everyone

Every person could have a key. The head of financials, the individuals, the teachers. Threshold signatures are the fix for the mess: quorum-based, not unanimous.

## The design boundary

A playground having delayed funding for an extra week isn't critical. It's not like you're talking about medical supplies. Average strike lasts a couple of days. Friction is affordable where urgency is low.
