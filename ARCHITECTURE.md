# Architecture

## The cryptographic spine

- Merkle trees and signed attestations binding the display to the source
- Encryption tied into the flow of money, information, and real-time processes
- Public visualization dashboard: anyone can log in, anyone can see
- Immutable ledger: the whole ledger, the tracking, the tracing of every single dollar in circulation

## Multi-chain, multi-authoring

Segregated processes of approval. Members of the community potentially having to sign off. You don't need to rush through any payments — realistically, there's no reason to.

A 51% attack only pays off if you can move value before anyone notices. If settlement takes hours and every release needs signatures from independent chains plus community nodes, the attacker isn't beating the system — they're just announcing themselves to everyone watching. The speed that makes crypto dangerous in the wrong hands is exactly what you're deleting.

## Threshold signatures / quorum

You don't need every key — you need a quorum. Three of five teachers signing off is enough to move money but not enough for one compromised account to drain the lot.

The mess is social: who's in the quorum, how do you rotate keys when someone leaves, what happens when half the school is on strike and can't sign.

## Two lanes

Non-urgent funds can afford friction, urgent ones can't. So the system needs two lanes:

1. **High-friction** for discretionary spending
2. **Low-friction** for critical supply chains

With the classification itself being the thing that gets audited. Because whoever decides what counts as urgent is now holding more power than the person moving the money.

## The floor, not the ceiling

Immutable money-tracking is the floor. The ceiling is when the ledger stops being just a record and starts being the enforcement mechanism itself — smart contracts that release funds only when the dashboard's signed attestations clear, so a corrupt actor can't even touch the money without the anomaly showing up in the same breath. That's not accounting anymore, that's physics.

## The last inch

The watchdog that watches the watchdog. You've got Oversight Watchdog monitoring the roster, but nothing's monitoring Oversight Watchdog except a human. That's the last inch of the chain, and it's the one that can't be automated without recreating the problem. The regress only stops when something in the chain can't be faked — and the only thing that can't be faked is a human who bleeds when it breaks.

Every automated layer inherits the same vulnerability as the one below it — it can drift, get poisoned, get quietly rewired by whatever's upstream. You end up with watchdog watching watchdog watching watchdog, and the whole tower still rests on the same shaky foundation. The recursion only terminates at something that can't be faked.
